# Decision log

Product and scope decisions for Film Darkroom, newest first. P1–P3 were made for BW
Darkroom and P4–P6 for Colour Darkroom, before the two were combined (P7). Model decisions
are in the design documents: black and white D1–D11 in
[design 0001](design/0001-negative-simulation-model.md#0-summary-of-decisions) and D12– in
[design 0002](design/0002-datasheet-calibration-and-paper.md); colour C1–C10 in
[design 0003](design/0003-colour-film-model.md#2-decisions); the combined app U1–U6 in
[design 0004](design/0004-one-app-two-processes.md#2-decisions).

| # | Date | Decision | Rationale |
|---|------|----------|-----------|
| P7 | 2026-11-04 | **Combine BW Darkroom and Colour Darkroom into Film Darkroom.** One code base (github.com/catskinroyale/film-darkroom, continuing BW Darkroom's history with Colour Darkroom's merged in) and one app with a Colour / B&W switch. The engines stay separate; core, raw, I/O, styles, the desktop shell and the CLI are shared (design 0004). Supersedes P4 and P5. Film Darkroom reads, but never writes, the two apps' styles and sidecars. | The user asked for one code base and one app to switch between colour and mono. The shared foundations had been copied (P5) and were near-identical, so every fix had to be made twice; the engines differ and stay apart. Reading the old apps' data keeps users' work, and not writing it keeps both old apps usable. |
| P6 | 2026-11-02 | **Colour stocks start illustrative; datasheet calibration follows.** Every colour film and print stock ships with parameters chosen by eye and labelled illustrative, in the About window, the curves panel and every export recipe. | A working colour pipeline with the right structure comes first. Kodak publishes per-layer curves, sensitivities and dye spectra for most of these stocks, so they can be fitted one at a time (design 0003 §4) without changing the model. |
| P5 | 2026-11-02 | *Superseded by P7.* **Copy, don't share, BW Darkroom's foundations** for Colour Darkroom. | The two apps were to evolve independently; a shared crate could be extracted later. P7 did that. |
| P4 | 2026-11-02 | *Superseded by P7.* **Colour Darkroom is a separate app from BW Darkroom**, with its own name, settings, styles and sidecars. | The user first asked for a colour app separate from the black-and-white one, then for one combined app. Separate sidecars meant both apps could edit the same raw files; P7 keeps that by reading but never writing them. |
| P3 | 2026-10-24 | **Prints are exposed for the shadows.** The printer exposes so the scene's zone I prints at the density normal development gives it, replacing "hold the black point" (clear film). Normal development prints as before. | With films fitted to Kodak's curves, a push adds so much density (and real shadow speed) that prints holding clear film came out 1½ stops too light, highlights blown. Printing for the shadows is real darkroom practice and keeps a push's effect as more contrast with brighter mid-tones and highlights. Chosen by the user over keeping the black point or re-anchoring mid-grey. |
| P2 | 2026-10-24 | **Calibrate from manufacturer datasheets, read exactly from their PDFs; the paper is Kodak Polymax II RC.** Curves are taken from datasheets' vector paths (design 0002), starting with Tri-X + D-76 (Level 2) and Kodak's Polymax II RC grade family, which replaces the placeholder paper. Other films stay illustrative until fitted. | Datasheet curves are the best published data and reading the vectors removes digitising error. Kodak's variable-contrast papers (Polymax II, Polycontrast IV) publish their curves as vectors but are discontinued; Ilford's current papers publish raster images only. Polymax II has the fullest grade family (−1 to 5+). Ideas came from NegPy (GPL-3.0); its code and fitted constants are not used. |
| P1 | 2026-10-23 | **The digital look is deferred. Work concentrates on the core use case: film, chemicals and paper.** The digital look is a separate output that would map paper Dmax to screen black, offer contrast beyond paper grades, expose night scenes automatically and take the starting brightness from the camera. It stays planned but unscheduled. Until it is revisited, new work goes to the film, developer, negative and print models and the controls for them. | The physically grounded darkroom pipeline is what sets the app apart, and it still has gaps: film and developer parameters are illustrative, and the Tri-X + D-76 fit is not done. A digital look would be a deliberate departure from that model, so it should stay separate and be labelled as such when it comes. |
